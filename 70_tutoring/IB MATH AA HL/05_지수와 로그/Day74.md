1. Given $2^x = A$ and $2^y = B$, use the laws of exponents to express $A^3 \times B^2$ as a power of $2$. Based on this result, use the definition of a logarithm to express $\log_2 (A^3 B^2)$ in terms of $x$ and $y$.
    
      
    
2. Let $k = \log_2 7$. Convert this equation into an exponential form using the definition of a logarithm, and take the base-10 logarithm of both sides to demonstrate how the change-of-base formula is derived.
    
      
    
3. Take the base-3 logarithm of both sides of $x = 2^{\log_3 5}$. Simplify the right side by using the property that moves the exponent of the argument to the front, and then convert it back into an exponential form to prove that $2^{\log_3 5} = 5^{\log_3 2}$.
    
      
    
4. Let $p = \log_8 16$. Instead of applying formulas directly, convert the expression into $8^p = 16$ using the definition of a logarithm. Unify the base on both sides to $2$, and solve the resulting exponential equation to find the value of $p$.
    
      
    
5. Let $a = \log_2 3$ and $b = \log_3 32$. Convert both equations into their exponential forms, and substitute one into the other to find the value of $a \times b$.

Prove each of the following statements using mathematical induction.

Prove that for all positive integers $n$:

$$1 + 2 + 3 + \dots + n = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$$

Prove that for all integers $n \ge 4$:

$$2^n > n^2$$

Prove that for any real number $x > -1$ and all integers $n \ge 1$, the following inequality holds:

$$(1+x)^n \ge 1+nx$$

Prove that for all positive integers $n$, the following inequality holds:

$$1 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + \dots + \frac{1}{2^n} \ge 1 + \frac{n}{2}$$
