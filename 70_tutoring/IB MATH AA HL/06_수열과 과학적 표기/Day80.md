1. Write $45\,000\,000$ in the form $a \times 10^k$, where $1 \le a < 10$ and $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.
    
      
    
2. Write $0.000\,308$ in scientific notation.
    
      
    
3. Calculate $(2.0 \times 10^4) \times (3.5 \times 10^3)$, expressing your answer in scientific notation.
    
      
    
4. Evaluate $\frac{7.2 \times 10^{-2}}{9.0 \times 10^{-6}}$ and give your answer in scientific notation.
    
      
    
5. Calculate $(4.5 \times 10^5) + (3.2 \times 10^4)$ without using a calculator, giving your answer in the form $a \times 10^k$, where $1 \le a < 10$ and $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.
    
      
    
6. Calculate $(8.0 \times 10^{-4}) - (9.0 \times 10^{-5})$, expressing your answer in scientific notation.
    
      
    
7. Given that $P = 4.0 \times 10^6$ and $Q = 8.0 \times 10^3$, evaluate $\sqrt{\frac{P}{Q}}$ in scientific notation.
    
      
    
8. Evaluate $\frac{(1.2 \times 10^7) + (8.0 \times 10^6)}{4.0 \times 10^{-3}}$ and write the final result in scientific notation.
    
      
    
9. A cube has a side length of $2.5 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}$. Find its volume in $\text{m}^3$, expressing the answer in standard scientific notation $a \times 10^k$.
    
      
    
10. An arithmetic sequence has first term $u_1 = 5$ and common difference $d = 3$. Find the 20th term, $u_{20}$.
    
      
    
11. The first three terms of a geometric sequence are $4, 12, 36$. Find the common ratio $r$ and the 6th term, $u_6$.
    
      
    
12. An arithmetic sequence is given by $19, 15, 11, 7, \dots$. Find the first negative term of this sequence.
    
      
    
13. In an arithmetic sequence, $u_4 = 14$ and $u_9 = 34$. Find the first term $u_1$ and the common difference $d$.
    
      
    
14. The third term of a geometric sequence is $12$ and the sixth term is $-96$. Find the common ratio $r$ and the first term $u_1$.
    
      
    
15. The terms $k-2$, $k+1$, and $3k-1$ are three consecutive terms of an arithmetic sequence. Find the value of $k$.
    
      
    
16. Three consecutive terms of a geometric sequence with positive terms are $x-1$, $x+2$, and $3x$. Find the exact value of $x$.
    
      
    
17. The first term of an arithmetic sequence is 2, and the common difference is $d \neq 0$. The 1st, 4th, and 13th terms of this sequence form three consecutive terms of a geometric sequence. Find the value of $d$.
    
      
    
18. In a geometric sequence, the 2nd term is $\ln 2$ and the 4th term is $\ln 16$. Given that all terms are positive, express the common ratio $r$ in simplest form.
    
      
    
19. An arithmetic sequence has first term $u_1 = 4$ and common difference $d = 5$. Find the sum of the first 12 terms, $S_{12}$.
    
      
    
20. In an arithmetic series, the first term is 3 and the last term (the 10th term) is 47. Calculate the sum of these 10 terms.
    
      
    
21. A geometric sequence has first term $u_1 = 2$ and common ratio $r = 3$. Find the sum of the first 5 terms, $S_5$.
    
      
    
22. Evaluate the sum of the arithmetic series: $\sum_{k=1}^{20} (3k - 2)$.
    
      
    
23. The sum of the first $n$ terms of an arithmetic sequence is given by $S_n = 2n^2 + 5n$. Find the first term $u_1$ and the common difference $d$.
    
      
    
24. For a geometric series, $u_1 = 3$ and $r = 2$. Find the smallest integer $n$ such that $S_n > 100$.
    
      
    
25. The sum of the first $n$ terms of an arithmetic series is 140. Given that the first term is 2 and the common difference is 3, find the value of $n$.
    
      
    
26. An arithmetic sequence has $u_1 = \log_2 3$ and common difference $d = \log_2 3$. Show that the sum of the first $n$ terms can be written in the form $\log_2(3^{f(n)})$, and state the expression for $f(n)$.
    
      
    
27. In a geometric sequence of positive real numbers, the sum of the first two terms is 12, and the sum of the first four terms is 120. Find the first term $u_1$ and the common ratio $r$.
    
      
    
28. The first term of an infinite geometric sequence is 16 and the common ratio is $\frac{1}{2}$. Find the sum to infinity, $S_\infty$.
    
      
    
29. State whether the infinite geometric series $9 - 6 + 4 - \dots$ converges. If it converges, find its sum to infinity.
    
      
    
30. An infinite geometric series has a common ratio $r = -\frac{1}{3}$ and its sum to infinity is 12. Find the first term $u_1$.
    
      
    
31. The sum to infinity of a geometric sequence is 27, and the sum of the first two terms is 24. Given that $0 < r < 1$, find the common ratio $r$ and the first term $u_1$.
    
      
    
32. Find the exact fractional representation of the recurring decimal $0.2\dot{7} = 0.2777\dots$ using the sum to infinity of a geometric series.
    
      
    
33. The first term of an infinite geometric series is 4, and the second term is $2\ln x$. Find the range of values of $x$ for which the series converges.
    
      
    
34. The sum to infinity of a geometric series is 18. The second term is 4. Find all possible values of the common ratio $r$ and the corresponding first term $u_1$.
    
      
    
35. An infinite geometric series is given by $\sum_{n=0}^\infty (3 - 2\log_2 x)^n$. Find the range of values of $x$ for which this series converges.
    
      
    
36. In an infinite geometric sequence with positive terms, each term is equal to twice the sum of all subsequent terms (i.e., $u_k = 2\sum_{n=k+1}^\infty u_n$ for all $k \ge 1$). Find the common ratio $r$ of the sequence.
    
      
    
37. Simplify the expression $\frac{x^7 \cdot x^{-2}}{(x^2)^3}$, writing your answer with a positive exponent.
    
      
    
38. Evaluate the exact numerical value of $27^{-\frac{2}{3}}$ without using a calculator.
    
      
    
39. Simplify $(16a^4 b^{-8})^{\frac{3}{4}}$, giving your answer without negative exponents.
    
      
    
40. Simplify $\frac{\sqrt{32} \times 2^{n-1}}{4^{n+1}}$, expressing your answer in the form $2^k$ in terms of $n$.
    
      
    
41. Given that $\frac{9^{2x-1}}{27^{x+1}} = 3^{k}$, find an expression for $k$ in terms of $x$.
    
      
    
42. Simplify the algebraic fraction: $\frac{2^{x+3} - 2^{x+1}}{2^{x+2}}$.
    
      
    
43. Solve for real $x$: $4^x - 3 \cdot 2^{x+1} - 16 = 0$.
    
      
    
44. Simplify completely: $\frac{x^{\frac{1}{2}} - 4x^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{x^{\frac{1}{2}} - 2}$.
    
      
    
45. Given that $2^a = 3$ and $3^b = 5$, find the exact numerical value of $8^{ab} + 4^a$.
    
      
    
46. Evaluate the exact value of $\log_3 54 - \log_3 2$.
    
      
    
47. Express $2\log_a 5 + \log_a 4$ as a single logarithm of the form $\log_a k$.
    
      
    
48. Given that $\log_{10} 2 = p$ and $\log_{10} 3 = q$, express $\log_{10} 18$ in terms of $p$ and $q$.
    
      
    
49. Given that $\log_2 a + \log_4 b = 5$, express $b$ in terms of $a$.
    
      
    
50. Evaluate $\log_3 25 \times \log_5 27$ without using a calculator.
    
      
    
51. Given that $\ln(x^2 y) = 5$ and $\ln\left(\frac{x}{y}\right) = 1$, find the exact values of $\ln x$ and $\ln y$.
    
      
    
52. Given that $\log_a b = 3$, evaluate $\log_{\sqrt{a}}\left(\frac{a^2}{b^3}\right)$.
    
      
    
53. If $\log_2(x-1) - \log_4(x+5) = 1$, express this equation in a simplified algebraic form without logarithms.
    
      
    
54. Given that $a, b > 1$ and $\log_a b + 6\log_b a = 5$, find the two possible values of $\log_a b$. Hence, express $b$ in terms of $a$ for each case.

56. Solve for $x$: $\log_2(3x - 1) = 3$.
    
      
    
57. Solve for $x$: $4^{x+1} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{x-4}$.
    
      
    
58. Solve for $x$: $\log_3(x) + \log_3(x - 2) = 1$.
    
      
    
59. Solve for $x$: $2^{2x} - 5 \cdot 2^x + 4 = 0$.
    
      
    
60. Solve for $x$: $\ln(x+4) - \ln(x-1) = \ln 2$.
    
      
    
61. Solve for $x$: $9^x - 2 \cdot 3^{x+1} - 27 = 0$.

66. Solve $\ln(x^2) = 4$. State the domain of the expression $\ln(x^2)$ and list all valid real solutions.
    
      
    
67. Solve for $x$: $\log_4(x - 2) + \log_4(x - 5) = 1$. Clearly state all domain restrictions and show why any extraneous root is rejected.
    
      
    
68. Solve for $x$: $\log_3(x + 6) - \log_3(x) = 1$. State the domain restrictions and justify your final solution.
    
      
    
69. Consider the equation $\log_x(4x - 3) = 2$. State all domain restrictions on the base and the argument, solve the equation, and determine which solutions are valid.
    
      
    
70. Solve for $x$: $\log_2(x - 1) + \log_2(x + 1) = \log_2(3x + 9)$. Explicitly demonstrate how the domain conditions affect the final set of valid solutions.
    
      
    
71. Consider the equation $\ln(x - 2) + \ln(x - 4) = \ln(2x - 5)$. Find the common domain where all logarithmic terms are simultaneously defined, solve the equation, and explain why certain algebraic roots must be rejected.
    
      
    
72. Solve for $x$: $\log_{(x-1)}(x^2 - 2x - 3) = 1$. State all domain restrictions for the base and argument, and determine whether any valid solution exists.
